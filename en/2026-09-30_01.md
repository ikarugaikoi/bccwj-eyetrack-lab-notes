## 5. Observations and issues

**CD second-half validation failure.** Final right-eye four-point Accuracy averaged 0.797°. The upper-right value was 1.112° and the lower-right value was 1.267°, exceeding the worst-point criterion of 1.00°. The recording did not proceed to passage text.

**DC calibration and black-dot checks.** The final right-eye validation passed on the fourth attempt in the second half. Upper-right validation Precision SD was high in both halves: 2.091° and 1.682°, with corresponding Accuracy values of 0.542° and 0.344°. The black-dot check before the first practice passage passed after approximately 12.367 seconds. One reminder was given when it had not passed at ten seconds, in accordance with the established rule. All other verifiable black-dot checks passed within ten seconds.

**Practice keypresses.** Q was recorded on one text page during CD practice, followed by Space to advance. On the first question in DC practice, Space was pressed before P was used to answer. The specific reason is unknown; the operator suspected accidental keypresses.

**Monitor-height self-test.** The experimenter tested a 10 cm elevation, no elevation, and a 5.3 cm elevation, in that order, with three rounds per condition and two repetitions of the sequence. Accuracy was handwritten for eighteen rounds, including an additional far-upper-left calibration point. Round-by-round comparisons showed that handwritten rounds 1–7 correspond to software log attempts 1–7, and rounds 8–18 correspond to attempts 10–20. There was also an extra calibration not included in the handwritten table and a separate validation check triggered by accidentally selecting validation instead of recalibration. The upper-right validation point in handwritten round eleven (log attempt thirteen) was treated as missing for both eyes because the tester was distracted. See the [monitor-height and validation-error trend report](../reports/2026-09-30-display-height/en.md) for the detailed comparison.

The self-test primarily examined whether raising the monitor produced a clear difference. Simple calculations on site suggested that the benefit was mainly in right-eye four-point validation, while error at the additional far-upper-left calibration point increased. Checks using the software records showed that right-eye mean four-point validation error decreased by approximately 0.0993° (19.6%) at 5.3 cm and 0.0601° (11.8%) at 10 cm relative to no elevation. For the additional far-upper-left calibration point, the five retained native rounds gave a right-eye mean error of 0.8090° for two rounds without elevation and 0.9743° for three rounds at 5.3 cm, an increase of approximately 0.1653° (20.4%). The on-site judgment was that the overall benefit was not clear. Considering the lower accuracy near the screen edges, the monitor was not raised for the main study.
