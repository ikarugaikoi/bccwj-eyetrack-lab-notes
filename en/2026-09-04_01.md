## 2. Purpose and overview

This was the first end-to-end test of the eye-tracking reading workflow, intended to establish whether the reading procedure could be run from start to finish. Lexical decision, grammar testing, and other test components were not administered. After `BCCWJ_EyeTrack_PRACTICE_A_ZH` and `BCCWJ_EyeTrack_FORMAL_AB_H1_ZH`, `BCCWJ_EyeTrack_FORMAL_AB_H2_ZH` crashed when Design or Record was clicked. `BCCWJ_EyeTrack_FORMAL_CD_H2_ZH` was used as a temporary replacement, and a workflow test was completed.

Problems were found with the transition conditions and display of some screens. There were also instances in which the black-dot fixation step could not be passed; this improved after recalibration. The data had been exported but had not been analyzed at the time of documentation.

## 5. Observations and issues

**`BCCWJ_EyeTrack_FORMAL_AB_H2_ZH` crash.** No crashes occurred during checks on the local machine after project construction. During the September 4 test, the project itself could be opened, but clicking Design or Record produced a loading icon, followed by a crash. `BCCWJ_EyeTrack_FORMAL_CD_H2_ZH` was substituted on the spot, and the workflow test was continued and completed. The cause of the crash remained unknown at the time of documentation; any error message was not recorded.

**Fixation-duration screen-transition conditions remained on some screens.** This condition had not been disabled on individual screens. The experimenter confirmed that this was an oversight during manual construction. The affected screens, whether unintended transitions actually occurred, and the actions taken at the time were not recorded.

**Display errors on some screens.** Display errors were found on individual screens. The experimenter confirmed that these errors were also human errors introduced during manual construction. The affected screens, the specific display errors, screenshots, and the actions taken at the time were not recorded.

**Failure to pass the black-dot fixation step and interpretation concerning glasses.** During the experiment, the black-dot fixation step could not be passed, and the situation improved after recalibration. Based on this observation, the experimenter judged that movement of the participant's glasses relative to the bridge of the nose and actions such as adjusting the glasses had a substantial effect on gaze tracking in this test. The proposed sources of error are described in Section 7. The data had not been analyzed, so the mechanisms and magnitude of these effects had not been confirmed through data analysis.

## 7. Lessons learned

The following two points summarize lessons explicitly stated by the experimenter.

**1. Avoid repetitive manual work wherever possible.** The projects were constructed manually, and the omitted screen-transition settings and display errors were human errors. From this experience, the experimenter concluded that repetitive manual work "almost inevitably produces simple errors" and that repetitive manual operations should be reduced wherever possible.

**2. Pay attention to the effects of glasses shifting and being adjusted.** The experimenter judged that glasses shifting had a substantial effect on the eye-tracking experiment. Proposed sources of error include changes in refraction caused by glasses shifting, errors associated with the act of adjusting the glasses, and errors caused by changes in head position after adjustment. A chin rest was used in this test. The black-dot fixation step could not be passed on some occasions, and the situation improved after recalibration. These mechanisms are the experimenter's hypotheses and have not been confirmed through data analysis.
