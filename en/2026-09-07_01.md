## 5. Observations and issues

**1. The device had previously been assumed to support 250 Hz.** The experimenter had believed that the laboratory's Tobii Pro Fusion operated at 250 Hz, but subsequently confirmed that 120 Hz was used in this session. Existing review reports showed a saved sampling rate of 120 Hz in all three recordings, with no 250 Hz option in the device-reported list of available rates. The median interval between raw eye-tracking sample timestamps was approximately 8.333 ms, consistent with 120 Hz. The effect on the study's measurement outcomes remains to be assessed.

**2. The saved screen geometry did not match the monitor used on site.** During the subsequent review of Windows exports and native project backups, the `.rec` files for the practice, formal H1, and formal H2 recordings were inspected. All three contained the following settings:

| Field | Saved value | Aspect ratio |
|---|---|---|
| `RecordingDimensions` / `DisplayNativeResolution` | 1920 × 1200 px | 16:10 |
| `ScreenSize` | Approximately 527.04 × 296.46 mm | 16:9 |
| `CalibrationPlane` | The width and height defined by the corner points were also approximately 527.04 × 296.46 mm | 16:9 |

These millimeter dimensions came directly from the settings saved in the recording files. **They were neither filled in from specifications looked up using the monitor model nor measured on site.**

The monitor used on site was subsequently confirmed to be an EIZO ColorEdge CS2400R. Its manufacturer's nominal viewable image area is 518.4 × 324.0 mm, with a native resolution of 1920 × 1200 and a 16:10 aspect ratio. Compared with these specifications, the saved physical width was approximately 8.64 mm greater and the height approximately 27.54 mm smaller. These differences cannot be explained by decimal rounding.

The experimenter suspected that dimensions configured by another laboratory user for a 23.8-inch, 1920 × 1080 Dell monitor might have been retained. The origin of the settings has not been confirmed. The exact Dell model is scheduled to be checked at the laboratory on 2026-09-09.

**3. The display setup needs to be revised.** The experimenter reported finding a screen-size limit of "24.1 inches, 16:9" in Tobii Pro Eye Tracker Manager, judged that the EIZO CS2400R did not meet the requirements, and decided to change monitors. The experimenter further confirmed that the interface used the term `Maximum`. The full message and applicable mounting conditions have not been preserved in this log. The difference between the reported 24.1 inches and the 24 inches on the official website below remains to be checked.

Reference check: The [Tobii Pro Fusion official product specifications](https://www.tobii.com/products/eye-trackers/screen-based/tobii-pro-fusion), consulted on 2026-09-08, list an optimal screen size of up to 24 inches with a 16:9 aspect ratio (`Optimal screen size`).

## 7. Lessons learned

1. **Check the actual sampling rate and available maximum of the specific device before the experiment.** Cross-check the device interface, recording metadata, and actual sampling intervals.
2. **Check both screen resolution in pixels and physical display dimensions.** After changing the monitor on shared equipment, recheck the screen geometry saved in the eye-tracking system.
3. **Include equipment and data-quality checks in the pilot.** Alongside workflow operation, check equipment compatibility, screen geometry, and calibration / validation results.
